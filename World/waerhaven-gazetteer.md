# WAERHAVEN
### Sundermere Gazetteer, Volume Two — a city of the Ythan League

26,788 souls. 2,365 roofs. Eight wards, three gates, one chain.

---

## 1. The Ground

Waerhaven stands on a promontory pushing north into the Ythan Mere, with fresh water on three sides and deep forest on the fourth. This single fact explains the entire city.

**No river reaches Waerhaven.** Not one. Every log that becomes a hull is cut in Duskhollow, forty miles south, and hauled overland on ox-roads and timber-slides to Hallowgate. It is a slow, expensive, ruinous way to run a shipyard, and Waerhaven does it anyway, because the alternative is not building ships — and the Ythan League has a fleet instead of an army.

The harbour is a west-facing bay bitten into the promontory's flank, sheltered from everything except a north-westerly, which is the wind that matters. Its mouth is 290 metres across and can be closed by a forged chain slung between two towers. Eleven men on the capstan, four minutes, and nothing gets in or out.

There is no road north. The nearest settlement in that direction is in Drennmark, eighty-five miles across forest with no track worth the name. Waerhaven's only land connection to the world is the Hallowgate road, running fifty-five miles south-west to Venddock and on to Lathmere.

**In short:** a shipyard city with no river, a fortress with one landward gate, and a harbour it can lock. It is enormously defensible and utterly dependent on a single road.

### Old Waer
Four centuries older than the charter, and it has never forgiven the charter for arriving. Lanes too narrow for a cart, houses built against houses, and a population that still calls itself *Waerfolk* rather than League. The point's light burns at the top of it.

*Fish. Woodsmoke. Somebody always singing badly.*

### Highstrand
Stone houses, glazed windows, and the three buildings that argue over who runs the city: the Wet Hall, the Shipwrights' Moot, and the Factor's House. From the Moot Yard you can see every slip, every pier, and forty miles of open Mere.

*Quiet. Swept. Watched from upper windows.*

### The Boom
Waerhaven's reason for existing. Five stone piers, a double rank of League warehouses, and a hand-forged chain across the harbour mouth. It has been raised in anger twice in ninety years. It has been raised for drill every single week of those ninety years, because the Boom Watch is paid by the drill.

*Tar, wet rope, sawdust, cold water. Gulls. Shouting.*

### The Slipways
Six building slips, a dry dock, the mast pond, and a ropewalk 380 metres long — the longest continuous roofed structure on the continent, and the only reason Waerhaven matters more than Venddock. Every hull in the Ythan fleet was laid down here.

*The ox-mill grinding. Adzes. Steam from the timber-bending kilns.*

### Hallowgate Ward
Where the ox-roads arrive. Timber yards stacked four metres high, the carters' yards, the Watch, the granary, and Hallowmarket — the only place in the city where you can buy everything and trust nothing.

*Oxen, dust, the smell of green sawn pine.*

### Tarrow
Downwind by law, since the fire of the ninth year. The pitch yards, the boom forge, and the oakum sheds where the poor pick old rope apart until their fingers split. Waerhaven cannot float a hull without Tarrow and cannot stand to look at it.

*Black smoke, black hands, a smell that gets into cloth and stays.*

### The Wardenswatch
Six years ago there was nothing here but the lazar house. Now there are perhaps nineteen hundred people from Drennmark living outside the wall, in a suburb the League's charter says does not exist. Waerhaven feeds it because Waerhaven needs the hands.

*Too many people, not enough roofs, and everyone facing north.*

### The North Horn
A spit of rock with a tower on it, the north anchor of the boom chain, and forty fisher-huts that pay no tax to anyone because nobody has ever been able to decide whose they are.

*Wind. Always wind.*

## 3. Who Actually Runs It

Nominally, Waerhaven is a chartered city of the Ythan League, administered by a Factor appointed from Lathmere.

Actually, it is run by an argument between three bodies, none of which has clear authority over the others:

| Power | Influence | Seat |
|---|---|---|
| **The Shipwrights' Moot** | ◆◆◆◆◆ | The Shipwrights' Moot |
| **The Tarrow Houses** | ◆◆◆◆◇ | The Pitch Yards |
| **The League Factor** | ◆◆◆◇◇ | The Factor's House |
| **The Boom Watch** | ◆◆◆◇◇ | Hallowgate Keep |
| **The Quiet Charter** | ◆◆◆◇◇ | *a door with no sign* |
| **The Grey Wardens** | ◆◆◇◇◇ | The Grey Hall |
| **The Waerfolk** | ◆◆◇◇◇ | The Fish Hall |

**The Shipwrights' Moot** holds the real power, because it can halt the League's fleet by folding its arms. Everyone knows this. Nobody has said it aloud yet. Master Shipwright **Gedda Halloway** — sixties, deaf in one ear from a lifetime of caulking hammers — has quietly concluded that the League cannot be relied on, and is deciding what to do about it.

**Factor Bevis Culm** has the writ, a small staff, and no friends. He is also eleven months into a lie about the granary that he cannot now unsay. He would take a great deal of help from anyone who offered it, and would owe them everything.

**The Tarrow Houses** — three families under Old Mother Reth — hold pitch, tar, oakum and the four hundred desperate workers who process them. Reth genuinely believes she is the only person in Waerhaven who feeds the poor, and she is very nearly right.

**Watch-Captain Oren Skeld** takes money from three parties and lets refugees through the gate for free, which he considers balances the account. It does not, but he sleeps.

**The Waerfolk** hold no office and settle most disputes in the city before they reach anyone who does. **Goodwife Ellsa Waer** is eighty-one and says the Mere "has gone quiet in the wrong way." She is not being poetic.

## 4. The Situation

Waerhaven is the Ythan League's closest city to Drennmark. For six years, people have been coming south out of the Wyrdpines.

The League's charter forbids the city to take them. The slipways cannot meet the League's fleet order without them.

So Waerhaven takes them, quietly, and everybody involved has a reason not to notice: the Moot needs hands, the Watch is paid not to look, the Tarrow Houses want cheap labour for the oakum sheds, and the Factor cannot report the problem without also reporting that his granary is two-thirds empty.

The result is a city running an illegal population it depends on, held together by a set of mutually assured embarrassments. It works. It is eleven weeks of grain away from not working.

And underneath that, quieter: three northern charts have gone missing, the Boom Forge is making something that is not chain, nine masts are unaccounted for, and a tenth hull class has been painted on a tavern beam by somebody who will not admit it.

**Somebody in Waerhaven is building a ship the League did not order, for a voyage north that the Charthouse has stopped admitting is possible.**

### A few worth knowing

**The Wet Hall** (8) — the League's hall, built on piles over a tidal cut so its ground floor floods twice a day *by design*: the founders' joke about where League power actually comes from.

**The Underquay** (26) — flooded cellars beneath the old quay, connected, and useful to people who dislike doors. Floods to the ceiling twice a day.

**The Mast Pond** (33) — great pines lie submerged two years to season. Counted once a year. Last autumn's count was short by nine, and was not repeated.

**The Temple of the Deep Mere** (4) — offerings go into the water, not onto an altar. For six years they have been washing back ashore. Sister Nairn has been quietly collecting them and storing them, because throwing them in again feels like an insult to something.

## 7. Rumours

Twelve, all true in some useful sense:

1. The Waer Light was dark four nights this year, and each night a boat came in unlogged.
2. The League's standard weight in the Counting Boom is four ounces light, and has been for six years.
3. Nine masts are missing from the Mast Pond and the count was never repeated.
4. The Boom Forge is making something behind a screen that is not chain.
5. Offerings thrown into the Mere have been washing back ashore since the year the Wyrdpines went bad.
6. Three charts of the northern shore walked out of the Charthouse in spring and were never reported.
7. Halvard the ironmonger has had no word from Karrun-Deep in eight months. Nor has anyone else.
8. Someone painted a tenth mast on the beam of the Nine Masts, and no shipwright will paint it out.
9. The granary holds eleven weeks of grain. The Factor has told Lathmere it holds twenty.
10. The Sunfather's brazier on the quay lit itself twice this spring with nobody near it.
11. Warden Vorn came south with sixty people and arrived with forty-one, and will not say what took the nineteen.
12. The dry dock is repairing a Drennmark boat for free, on nobody's authority.

## 8. Hooks

**The Short Weight** *(low)* — Ilber Cass will pay well to prove the Counting Boom's standard weight is light; he has been buying by that weight for six years and wants the loss back. Proving it means getting into a sealed box that three people can open, none of whom will.

**Nine Masts** *(low)* — Nine seasoned masts are gone from the Mast Pond and the Moot wants them found before the League asks. They are not stolen so much as *moved*, and where they went is a question about who is quietly building a hull nobody ordered.

**The Light Goes Out** *(mid)* — Four dark nights, four unlogged boats. The keeper of the Waer Light is being paid, or frightened, or replaced. Whoever is landing cargo on the point is not landing cargo.

**The Forty-One** *(mid)* — Aske Vorn's list names everyone who came south out of the Wyrdpines and where they came from. Two names on it are of people who are here in Waerhaven and should not be — because Vorn watched them die on the road.

**Eleven Weeks** *(mid)* — The granary is two-thirds empty and the Factor has lied about it to Lathmere. Winter is coming, the Wardenswatch has nineteen hundred extra mouths, and the League's grain barges stop the moment the lie is found. Someone has to fix this before it is discovered — or make sure it is discovered at exactly the right moment.

**What the Mere Gives Back** *(high)* — Sister Nairn has a locked room of offerings the Mere has returned. They are not weathered. Some are not the offerings that were thrown. The Temple wants it quiet, the Waerfolk want it answered, and something under the harbour is very patiently making a point.

**The Tenth Mast** *(high)* — A hull class that does not exist has been painted on the Nine Masts' beam, the Boom Forge is making something that is not chain, and three northern charts are missing. Somebody in Waerhaven is building a ship for a voyage the League has not authorised, to a shore the Charthouse has stopped admitting exists.

### waerhaven.json structure

```
meta        population, width 2400, height 1800, metresPerPixel 1.0,
            mapOffset [62,62], mapSize [3120,1924], settlementId, worldX/worldY
summary     seat, economy, government, defences, tension
districts[] id, name, sub, text, mood, centre, radius, colour, buildings
buildings[] x, y, w, d, rot, kind, district, id?, name?      (2,365 footprints)
streets[]   name, kind, w, pts[]                              (12 named routes)
locations[] id, key, name, type, x, y, district, desc, hook   (40 keyed places)
people[]    name, role, where -> location id, faction, note
factions[]  id, name, power 1-5, seat -> location id, desc, wants, leader
plazas / piers / slips / gates / towers / shore / curtain
rumours[] / hooks[]
```

City `x, y` are **metres** on the plan, origin at the plan's top-left. The rendered SVG places the plan at offset `mapOffset` inside the larger sheet, so to convert a city coordinate to a point on `waerhaven-map.svg`, add `[62, 62]`.
